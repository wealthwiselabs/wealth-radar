# Insurance — protection, not investment

Insurance is for protecting against catastrophic risk, not for building wealth.
Keep protection and investing separate.

## Life insurance

- **Don't use cash-value life insurance as an investment.** Indexed universal
  life (IUL) and variable universal life (VUL) policies are sold as investments,
  but their real returns are limited, and the illustrated returns are usually
  very misleading. For long-term growth it is generally better to invest the same
  money in a taxable brokerage account than to put it into these policies.
- **Narrow exception where a cash-value policy might be considered:** only when
  BOTH are true —
  1. you have already maximized your tax-advantaged contributions (see
     `tax-advantaged-accounts`) and still have a lump sum available to invest,
     AND
  2. you need to buy life insurance anyway.
- **Otherwise, buy term life insurance** for the coverage you need — it is cheap,
  pure protection — and invest the difference rather than paying for a permanent
  cash-value policy.

## Disability insurance

- **Cover disability if your employer doesn't.** If your employer does not
  provide disability insurance, it's a good idea to buy it yourself — especially
  for the income earner(s) your family depends on. A lost paycheck from a
  disability can be more financially damaging than most other risks.

General education, not personalized or insurance advice — confirm specifics with
a licensed professional.
