# Tax-advantaged accounts

Maximize tax-advantaged accounts before (and alongside) ordinary taxable
investing — the tax savings compound over decades.

- **Core accounts:**
  - **401(k)** (or 403(b)/similar employer plan) — contribute at least enough to
    get the full employer match (free money), and ideally up to the annual max.
  - **Roth IRA** — after-tax contributions that grow and withdraw tax-free.
  - **HSA** (if on an HSA-eligible health plan) — triple tax advantage; can be
    invested and used as a long-term account.
- **Order of operations (typical):** capture the 401(k) match → max HSA → max
  Roth IRA → max the rest of the 401(k) → then taxable / after-tax.
- **After-tax and Roth conversions:** once the pre-tax and Roth limits are used,
  consider **after-tax 401(k) contributions**, and the **backdoor Roth IRA**
  (for high earners over the Roth income limit) and the **mega-backdoor Roth**
  (after-tax 401(k) → Roth) where the plan allows.
- **Contribute the max if you can;** contribution limits change yearly, so check
  the current year's limits.

General education, not personalized or tax advice — confirm specifics with a
tax professional.
